Meet Shiro.

A cute, smart, and friendly personal robot to brighten your desk and your day. Built from scratch with ESP32, sunboard, and custom firmware.

PROJECT: SHIRO-V1
Core Philosophy

A small friend with a big heart.

Shiro started as a simple idea: what if your desk companion could be more than just a smart speaker? What if it could show emotion, share your day, and help you focus, all while being incredibly cute?

Dynamic Eyes

Fluid, Mochi-style animations give Shiro a life-like and expressive face reacting to logic states.

Chronos Sync

Get notifications, navigation prompts, and reminders right on your desk via app integration.

Touch Sensor

A simple capacitive touch sensor (TTP223) lets you cycle through modes or react to notifications.

Open Hardware

Built entirely with common DIY components, sunboard framing, and open-source code.

FIRMWARE INSTALLER

Web Serial Flasher

Plug your ESP32 directly into your USB port. You can install the Shiro core firmware directly from your browser without using the Arduino IDE. (Requires Chrome/Edge).

DEV LOGS

From Idea to Prototype

Documenting the trial, error, and hardware iterations.

LOG 01: SIMULATION

Wokwi Engine

It all began as code. Before any hardware, Shiro's first "heartbeat" was in a Wokwi simulation, testing basic logic and eye movements.

  • > Idea validation and concept testing
  • > Basic I/O and logic flow mapping
  • > Initial C++ eye animation routines
LOG 02: BREADBOARD

Arduino & Eyes

The first physical prototype! We moved the code to an Arduino and hooked up an I2C OLED screen. Shiro could finally "blink" in the real world.

  • > First working hardware prototype
  • > OLED display buffer rendering
  • > Hardcoded emotional expressions
LOG 03: UPGRADE

ESP32 Shift

Shiro got a brain upgrade. Moving to the ESP32 allowed for WiFi, Bluetooth, and much smoother "Deasi Mochi" style animations. Connected to the Chronos app for real-time data.

  • > Migrated architecture for processing power
  • > Fluid, dynamic geometry animations
  • > BLE/WiFi App Synchronization
LOG 04: INTERFACE

UI & Touch

Designed a clean UI for notifications, navigation, and time blocks. A capacitive touch sensor was added to the chassis for direct physical interaction.

  • > OLED UI overlays (Time, Nav, Alerts)
  • > TTP223 touch sensor wiring
  • > State-machine gesture logic
LOG 05: CHASSIS

Physical Body

With the electronics finalized, it was time to give Shiro a home. The first body was built from sunboard—a simple, effective, and easily modifiable design.

  • > Hand-cut foam sunboard housing
  • > Component mounting & cable management
  • > Fully standalone prototype achieved
